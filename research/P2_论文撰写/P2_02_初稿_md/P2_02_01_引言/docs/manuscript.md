# Introduction

Agent-assisted research workflows are becoming capable enough to plan experiments, call tools, coordinate multiple roles, and draft paper-like artifacts. Representative systems such as ReAct and Reflexion show how language agents can interleave reasoning, action, and feedback; AutoGen shows how configurable conversational agents can collaborate; MLAgentBench and the AI Scientist push further toward machine-learning experimentation and automated scientific writing [react2022; reflexion2023; autogen2023; mlagentbench2023; aiscientist2024]. This progress makes a practical engineering problem unavoidable: a workflow may produce plausible files, figures, reviews, and prose while the actual research truth becomes difficult to audit. In a paper pipeline, the failure is not merely that an agent might make a mistake. The deeper failure is that a manuscript claim, its evidence, its protocol gate, a reviewer objection, the author's response, and any negative or rejected result can drift apart while the system still appears productive.

Existing agentic research systems motivate this problem but do not by themselves close it. Tool-using and feedback-driven agents can generate useful trajectories, yet those trajectories do not define when a manuscript claim is supported. Multi-agent frameworks can organize roles, but role composition does not determine whether a phase transition has preserved evidence boundaries. Experiment-automation and paper-generation systems can create impressive end-to-end artifacts, but an artifact that looks like a paper still needs durable records showing which claims are supported, weak, unsupported, or blocked. Reporting and peer-review policies from venues such as Nature Portfolio and IEEE reinforce the need for transparent data, code, protocols, originality, disclosure, and review boundaries, but they are policies rather than a node-local operating procedure for agentic research [nature_reporting; ieee_peer_review_policy].

The concrete gap addressed here is therefore narrower than "agents for science" and more operational than "better paper writing." The cited representative systems demonstrate tool use, multi-agent coordination, or automated scientific writing, but they do not show a node-local operating procedure that binds each manuscript claim to evidence, protocol gates, independent review, response coverage, and explicit negative-result handling before phase transition. This gap is measurable. Under a fixed node set and budget, the relevant question is whether a governed workflow can improve claim-evidence validity, reduce unsupported claims, preserve negative evidence, and close reviewer-response loops relative to manual checklist, prompt-only, or ungated agent baselines. The current manuscript does not claim that this comparison has already produced a final performance result; it uses the comparison as the organizing protocol for a specialist engineering paper.

We propose an evidence-governed human-agent research workflow in which node closure, cross-phase claim identity, independent review, negative evidence, and formal-result eligibility are inspectable before manuscript claims are upgraded. The contribution has four bounded parts. First, a node-level evidence-governance contract treats a research node as a fail-able work unit whose closure depends on claim, evidence, review, and response state rather than file presence. Second, a cross-phase claim-evidence-protocol chain keeps proposal aims, method claims, result rows, figure callouts, reviewer critiques, and response actions tied to shared identifiers. Third, an independent review and negative-result gate separates author exit from node close so that unresolved hard failures, low scores, reject evidence, and blocked rows stay visible. Fourth, a formal evidence eligibility gate prevents provider-backed execution logs, synthetic/offline sanity checks, or data availability from becoming main-result claims until provider policy, data alignment, artifact contracts, selected backend, repeated formal rows, and ablations pass.

The rest of the manuscript follows the style decision fixed in P2_01: a specialist engineering IMRAD narrative, with an IEEE-style technical compression as backup and Nature-style broad-interest writing used only as a clarity lens. The preliminary section defines node lifecycle and evidence vocabulary. The workflow section gives the claim/evidence path and figure provenance rules. The methods section explains the node-local contract, review gate, response closure, negative-result retention, and PHM-Vibench to PHMGA responsibility boundary. The experiments and discussion section reports evidence by maturity: workflow-governance tables when row-level evidence exists, a limited synthetic/offline sanity-check row only under its boundary label, formal PHMGA/Vibench eligibility rows as blocked or future-required until every gate passes, and negative or unclear evidence as first-class table entries rather than hidden limitations.

This introduction intentionally stops short of final-result language. It does not claim submission readiness, final PHMGA performance, selected-backend success, RM101 resolution, or Stage C/D completion. Those unresolved conditions are carried forward as formal-evidence and reproducibility gates, not as introduction-level achievements. This boundary is the paper's central discipline: the manuscript may argue for an auditable governance workflow, but it may not turn an organized research story into a completed empirical result before the evidence ledger supports that upgrade.
