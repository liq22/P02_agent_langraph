# PHMGraph literature review and contribution boundary

Search date: 18 September 2026. This is a focused related-work review, not an exhaustive systematic review. The 38 distinct works below are all cited in `paper/draft/main.md`; the active bibliography is `phmgraph_review_2026.bib`. Publisher pages, conference proceedings and author manuscripts are the evidence sources. The reading-depth column distinguishes abstract-level positioning from inspected methods and ablations. An abstract is never used to infer that a paper did not study a mechanism. Preprint versions are identified in the bibliography; no publication status is inferred from a title or citation count.

## Synthesis: background → problem → closest work → motivation

**Background.** PHM research supplies numerical representations, diagnostic procedures and measured fault datasets. Scientific agents can coordinate such computations. The unresolved quantity in this manuscript is how a fixed agent actually uses a common set of capabilities to deliver a diagnosis.

**Closest work.** StateFlow already develops state-driven workflows and includes refined reactive and state-removal controls. PHMForge already studies industrial orchestration, failure categories and tool-level controls. TimeSage-MT already compares skill-guided reasoning with a structured pipeline and discusses a grounding–flexibility trade-off. Consequently, neither a state graph nor the observation that workflows can hurt is claimed as new.

**Specific question.** Under identical procedural content, what is the effect of an explicit active-block pointer with global tools, and what additional effect follows from progress gating? The gate bundles schema reduction, implicit phase information and executable restrictions. Its effect is conditional on the shared catalog and the supported PHM task, not a pure topology effect.

**Motivation and challenges.** A gate can prevent premature submission yet close useful post-prediction computation. The experiment must separate instruction localization from new knowledge, identify the actions actually removed, and retain first-attempt delivery failures. A valid transition is not itself a correct diagnosis. The current diagnosis task has one distinct raw window, so a second-independent-observation explanation is unsupported.

## What the current three contributions can claim

1. A fixed-content, two-component PHM control formulation: active-instruction indexing and progress-gated tool exposure.
2. An application-specific opportunity–selection analysis and explicit execution boundaries; the Bellman identity and finite-state representation are established foundations, not new general theorems.
3. Two paired task-primary first-attempt contrasts, with delivery failures and bearing dependence retained. Diagnostic effects remain unestimated until matched PHM outcomes exist.

This positioning is narrower than the earlier long-horizon recovery narrative. Its value must ultimately be established by the paired outcomes and observed activation of the restrictions. Adding references does not turn a protocol or a source-code property into an empirical contribution.
